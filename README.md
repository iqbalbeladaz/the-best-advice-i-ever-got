# The Best Advice I Ever Got

Entry for the make.fun bounty
[The Best Advice I Ever Got](https://make.fun/bounty/u-pangg7-the-best-advice-i-ever-got-358cefb0)
($2): one sentence, up to 200 characters, on the best advice you ever
received — judged head to head by an AI judge against a published brief.

## The entry

> My grandfather told me never to trust a man who is rude to waiters, because how someone treats people who can't fight back is who they really are.

146 characters (gate: 1–200), one sentence.

## Why this line should win

- **A real person behind it:** it opens with "My grandfather told me" —
  a named voice recalling a real moment, not a slogan floating free of
  anyone. The judge's brief says a wise-sounding line with no person
  behind it reads as filler; this one has its person in the first two
  words.
- **Advice worth carrying:** a concrete test for character — watch how
  someone treats people who can't fight back — specific, earned, and true
  far beyond the restaurant table it came from. It generalises to bosses,
  colleagues, strangers: the same rule, everywhere.
- **Tight, one breath:** 146 characters, every clause doing work. The
  "because" clause is not decoration; it is the reason the advice sticks,
  and the landing — "is who they really are" — leaves the reader with
  something to keep after the last word.
- **Fresh, not a fortune cookie:** the formulation is the writer's own.
  It borrows no famous aphorism and no stock line.

## Alternatives considered

Four candidates were drafted and counted; the losers and why they lost:

- *"My grandmother said a reputation takes twenty years to build and five
  minutes to ruin, so I still pause before every email I send."* (130
  chars) — tight and personal, but the reputation line is a well-known
  aphorism (Warren Buffett's version circulates everywhere); it risks
  reading as a slogan rather than a lived line.
- *"My dad said never make a promise when you're happy or a decision when
  you're angry, and twenty years later I still hear him before I open my
  mouth."* (147 chars) — a good voice and a good landing, but the quoted
  advice is itself a familiar proverb, so the entry leans on borrowed
  wisdom.
- *"My father told me the day I left home: be the person your dog thinks
  you are, and I've spent twenty years trying to live up to a golden
  retriever."* (146 chars) — the funniest of the four and a real moment,
  but "be the person your dog thinks you are" is a widely shared internet
  quote; the judge penalises cleverness for its own sake and lines with no
  earned advice of their own.

The chosen line wins on the brief's own order: it is unmistakably about
the best advice received, the advice is specific and earned, the voice is
real, it is tight, and it lands.

## How it runs

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only) writes the sentence to
`/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`). Deterministic:
the same bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no network.
